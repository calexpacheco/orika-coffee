Technical Case

Scroll doesn't move the page. It moves time.

Seven scenes, thirteen clips, one camera — no cut. How a static site of 300 lines of JavaScript sustains a continuous flight through an AI-generated world.

Vanilla JSVeo 3.1ffmpegVercel

The problem, and the premise

Tell the journey of a coffee without the visitor feeling they're jumping between sections. The navigation had to be continuous: one camera, crossing seven scenes.

The solution isn't to animate the camera in the browser. It's to pre-render the flight and let scroll control which instant of it you're in. The camera genuinely moved, at render time; scroll only drives the clock.

// the mapping, in one line
scroll (px) → active segment → video.currentTime → displayed frame
                  ↑ smoothed by requestAnimationFrame

It's the same technique behind Apple's product pages. It delivers a camera move that CSS or WebGL wouldn't reach with this finish — and the runtime cost is near zero: the browser just seeks a video.

Architecture: dives and connectors

Seven scenes, thirteen clips, chained into a single strand.

dive 1→connector 1→dive 2→connector 2→…→connector 6→dive 7
7 dives · 8s

Image-to-video. The camera starts outside the scene and flies inward, ending in the interior.

6 connectors · 8s

Frames-to-video. It rises, crosses the paper world and descends into the next scene.

Why this shape

It fits the miniature aesthetic. In a realistic walkthrough, the connector's pull-back would read as rewinding.

The core: frame-locked seams

It's what makes the stitch invisible — and the easiest mistake to make.

A connector's first and last frames must be the actually rendered frames of the neighboring dives, extracted from the video. Never the original scene image.

# extracting the seam frames
ffmpeg -sseof -0.15 -i dive_i.mp4    -frames:v 1 -q:v 2 last_i.png
ffmpeg -ss 0        -i dive_next.mp4 -frames:v 1 -q:v 2 first_next.png

# result: end(dive_i) == start(conn_i) and end(conn_i) == start(dive_i+1)

Why not use the original image: every generation renders slightly differently. If the connector ends on a fresh version of the scene and the next dive starts on its own, the two won't match — and a "pop" appears at the seam. A short crossfade covers minimal differences, but it's a safety net, not a substitute.

Playback: what usually goes wrong

Scrubbing frame by frame requires seekability, not keyframe density.

1 · Blob seeking

Many hosts don't serve HTTP range, which pins video.seekable to [0,0] and clamps every seek to frame 0 — the video looks frozen. The fix: fetch each clip as a Blob and play it from an in-memory object URL. A blob is always fully seekable, regardless of host.

2 · Don't trade quality for smoothness

All-intra bloats an 8s clip to ~25 MB. A short GOP solves it at about a third of the weight.

ffmpeg -i src.mp4 -an -vf "crop=1152:648,unsharp=5:5:0.8:5:5:0.0" \
  -c:v libx264 -preset slow -crf 20 -pix_fmt yuv420p \
  -g 8 -keyint_min 8 -sc_threshold 0 -movflags +faststart out.mp4

# the uniform crop across the 13 clips removes the watermark without off-centering

The audio layer

Since the video plays muted (it's scrubbed, not played back), any audio baked into the clip would be useless.

Continuous bed

One music track in the background, start to finish of the journey.

Per-scene sound

Seven looping ambiences, crossfading with the active section.

No engine changes

The active scene is detected by a MutationObserver on the class the engine already toggles.

Autoplay

Starts muted; only plays after an explicit visitor click.
